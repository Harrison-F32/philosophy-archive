c. 470–399 BCE · Ancient Greece

Socrates

The philosopher who made questioning himself a way of life.

Who Was Socrates?

Socrates was an Athenian philosopher whose life and conversations became foundational to Western philosophy. He wrote nothing himself, and much of what we know about him comes from writers such as Plato and Xenophon.

Rather than presenting a completed philosophical system, Socrates was famous for questioning people about concepts they believed they understood: justice, courage, virtue, knowledge, and the good life.

“The unexamined life is not worth living.”

The Socratic Method

Socrates often responded to confident claims with questions. Instead of simply telling someone that they were wrong, he would ask them to explain what they meant and then examine the consequences of their answer.

The result was often an unexpected realization: the person who thought they knew something discovered that their definition was incomplete or contradictory.

Major Ideas

Knowledge & Ignorance

Socrates is associated with recognizing the limits of one's own knowledge and examining one's beliefs.

Virtue

Socratic philosophy repeatedly asks what it means to live well and whether genuine virtue depends upon knowledge.

Questioning

Philosophical inquiry begins by examining claims that ordinary life often leaves unquestioned.

The Examined Life

For Socrates, reflection on one's beliefs and way of life was central to living well.

The Trial of Socrates

In 399 BCE, Socrates was tried in Athens on charges including impiety and corrupting the young. He was convicted and sentenced to death.

Plato's Apology presents Socrates' defense and offers one of the most famous portrayals of philosophical integrity in Western literature.
