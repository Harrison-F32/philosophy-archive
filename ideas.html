Ideas

Philosophy is less a collection of answers than a collection of questions. Explore the concepts that have shaped philosophical thought across centuries and traditions.

Questions & Concepts

The Absurd

What happens when the human desire for meaning confronts a universe that offers no obvious answer? Camus develops a philosophy of living and rebelling in the face of this tension.

Albert Camus

Nihilism

What happens when inherited values lose their authority? Nietzsche treats nihilism not simply as a conclusion, but as a crisis that must be confronted and potentially overcome.

Nietzsche · Kierkegaard

Existence

What does it mean to exist as an individual? Questions of choice, responsibility, anxiety, authenticity, and meaning become central to existential thought.

Kierkegaard · Nietzsche · Camus

Faith

Can faith involve a commitment that cannot be established through ordinary rational proof? Kierkegaard explores the tension between reason, individuality, and religious commitment.

Kierkegaard

Knowledge

What can we actually know, and what justifies a belief? From Socratic questioning to modern epistemology, knowledge remains one of philosophy's fundamental problems.

Socrates · Descartes · Locke

Language

How do words acquire meaning? Can language accurately represent reality? Frege and Russell transformed these questions into central problems of analytic philosophy.

Frege · Russell

Morality

What makes an action right or wrong? Philosophers have offered radically different answers, from virtue and reason to consequences, freedom, genealogy, and individual choice.

Aristotle · Mill · Nietzsche · de Sade

Justice

What does a just society look like? Political philosophers have debated equality, liberty, rights, authority, and the relationship between the individual and the state.

Plato · Locke · Mill · Rawls

Ideas rarely belong to one philosopher.

Philosophical traditions overlap, conflict, and develop in response to one another. An idea can change dramatically as it moves from one thinker to another. The Archive aims to show those connections rather than forcing philosophy into simple categories.
